## Reading (after the data, not pre-registered)

- **The budget is the cause of the 8B's wrong votes, not the model.** Raising it from 2048 to 8192 takes the share of
  voters whose first choice is not the nearest acceptable candidate from 60.4% to 12.0% and blanking against the rule
  from 47.3% to 11.1%, over 1,402 units from 355 logged calls; with no budget the figures are 10.2% and 9.8%. The paired
  counts are one-sided (E2 against 8192: 730 units the budget got wrong and 8192 right, 51 the other way). The noise floor
  (8-14% of units differ between two repeats of the same arm) is large, so the unit-level p-values are not to be taken
  at face value (units are clustered, repeats share a request), but a 48-point effect against a 10-point floor is not
  within it, and the cases that ended under the budget originally behave as they should (E2 6-16%, no trend with the
  budget: the arms differ by less than the floor).
- **What the pre-registered decision rule would have said without the noise guard**: E2 (8192) - E2 (none) = 1.8 points,
  within the 2 points, with a median reasoning cost below "none" (3,238 against 3,306 tokens), and 4096 is 14.7 points
  above "none": the candidate would be **8192**. The guard stopped it, so this is not a verdict.
- **Even with identical bytes the answer flips on about 10% of units** between two repeats, in every arm, at temperature 0:
  non-thinking batches were deterministic (OBS-020's finding), a thinking call on this server under load is not.
- **Cost.** 8192 doubles a vote call's latency (22 s to 44 s) and a chamber call's by 2.6 (22 s to 57 s). "None" is not an
  option for the chamber: 29% of its calls run into `max_tokens` (86 of 300) and fail.
- **The chamber's shifts depend on the budget.** The share of chamber units with a shift is 34.2% at 2048, 24.1% at 4096,
  18.7% at 8192, 20.7% with no budget (on the 1,062 units the unbudgeted arm managed to finish): cutting the reasoning
  makes the model shift more, so the chamber's motion in the runs we hold (OBS-004's update) is partly produced by the
  cut. There is no rule-truth for the chamber, so this says the shifts are budget-dependent, not which arm is right.
- **What this experiment did not test**: the president, nominee, coalition, forum and amendment turns (the budget binds on
  65-100% of the first four); any simulation; whether the answers at a larger budget change elections (the counted ballots
  are the kernel's, ADR-011, so the vote is an audit; a larger budget for an uncounted audit costs GPU time and buys a
  trustworthy instrument).
