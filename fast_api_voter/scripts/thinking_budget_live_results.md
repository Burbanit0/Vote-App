## Reading (after the data, not pre-registered)

- **A larger budget removes most of the 8B's wrong votes.** Raising it from 2048 to 8192 takes the share of voters whose
  first choice is not the nearest acceptable candidate from 60.4% to 12.0% and blanking against the rule from 47.3% to
  11.1%, over 1,402 units from 255 vote calls; with no budget the figures are 10.2% and 9.8%. The paired counts are
  one-sided (E2 against 8192: 730 units the budget got wrong and 8192 right, 51 the other way). Units are clustered in
  calls and the arms flip on 8-14% of units between repeats, so the p-values are optimistic, but a 48-point effect
  against a 10-point floor is not within it. The internal check holds: on the cases that ended under the budget
  originally the arms spread 9.6 points, inside the largest noise floor (13.8).
- **This shows the budget moves the answer, not that it is the whole cause**: at 8192 and with no budget, one voter in ten
  still picks wrong, which is the model's own error rate on this prompt.
- **What the decision rule gives depends on how rejected calls are scored; the registered verdict does not say.**
  With a rejected call scoring every voter wrong, E2 (8192) - E2 (none) is 1.8 points and the candidate is 8192. With
  rejected calls left out (the 24 voter-units of the 9 vote calls the unbudgeted arm lost), E2 (none) is 8.6% against
  11.3% for 8192 on the same units, a gap of 2.7 points above the 2-point tolerance, and no budget qualifies. Either
  way the noise guard stops it first, so neither is a verdict.
- **Even with identical bytes the answer flips on about 10% of units** between two repeats, in every arm: non-thinking
  batches were deterministic (OBS-020's finding), a thinking call on this server under load is not.
- **Cost.** 8192 doubles a vote call's latency (22 s to 44 s) and a chamber call's by 2.6 (22 s to 57 s). "None" is not an
  option for the chamber: 29% of its calls run into `max_tokens` (86 of 300) and fail.
- **The chamber's shifts depend on the budget.** On the 214 of 300 calls that finished in all four arms, the share of
  units with a shift is 31.6% at 2048, 25.6% at 4096, 20.1% at 8192, 20.7% with no budget: cutting the reasoning
  makes the model shift more, so part of the chamber's motion in the runs we hold (OBS-004's update) is produced by the
  cut. There is no rule-truth for the chamber, so this says the shifts are budget-dependent, not which arm is right.
- **The replay was at temperature 0.** The call log shows `temperature: null` because no per-call override was sent; the
  client then uses its configured value, which is 0.0 (the reference config; the config rejects anything else for batch
  decisions) with seed 42. So the 10% of units that flip between two repeats of identical bytes is not sampling
  temperature: it is the server's non-determinism of thinking calls (8 concurrent calls batched together, EAGLE-3). A
  serial replay would test that; it is the cheapest follow-up. Shifts per unit were not reported, only units with a shift.
- **Departures from the pre-registration**: the 8B baseline had 15 first-attempt vote calls, not 20; the log has no
  per-call temperature or seed, so every replay used the reference config's (0.0, seed 42); a rejected call is scored wrong
  in the primary table and left out in the sensitivity table, which the pre-registration did not say.
- **What this experiment did not test**: the president, nominee, coalition, forum and amendment turns (the budget binds on
  65-100% of the first four); any simulation; whether the answers at a larger budget change elections (the counted ballots
  are the kernel's, ADR-011, so the vote is an audit; a larger budget for an uncounted audit costs GPU time and buys a
  trustworthy instrument).
