## Population impact

**Who wanted to run.** At each of the nine presidential elections (years 0, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28) all 500 citizens were asked and exactly 170 said they wanted to run (0.34), the same count every time. Of those, 165 lost their party's nomination at each election (164 at years 2, 24 and 28; `nomination_lost`). Rupture-path candidates, who do not need a party nomination, also stand: the model's ranked ballots in the audit probe name 5 candidates at tick 0, 11 at tick 11, 7 at tick 16, 15 at tick 32, 13 at tick 48, 17 at ticks 64 and 80, 16 at tick 96 and 11 at tick 112, and four of the nine winners came by that path.

**Who turned out.** The journal records how many citizens abstained at each presidential election: 241 (t0), 323 (t11), 324 (t16), 373 (t32), 371 (t48), 408 (t64), 388 (t80), 364 (t96), 373 (t112), against 500 citizens. Abstention was highest at tick 64 and lowest at the first election.

**Who consulted what.** Pressure consultations range from 214 (the final tick only) to 1100 (year 28, 2.2 times the population, because citizens are asked at several ticks). Across the run the decided acts are overwhelmingly NOTHING; the years with 100% inaction are 4, 7 and 20. The decided SIGN_PETITION counts track the petitions closely: 121 decided vs 120 signed in year 0, 101 vs 99 in year 2, 124 vs 124 in year 3, 154 vs 151 in year 8. MOBILIZE peaked at 56 decided in year 3 and 46 in year 1; no event type in the digest shows what mobilization changed.

**How many signed.** 17 petitions were launched in all. By launch tick, with the highest signed ratio each reached: t0 0.242, t8 0.192, t11 0.258, t23 0.1, t32 0.304, t37 0.244, t45 0.202, t48 0.216, t56 0.05, t64 0.178, t72 0.172, t87 0.196, t95 0.01, t96 0.022, t104 0.226, t112 0.122, t120 0.07 (still open when the run ended). Only the t11 and t32 petitions crossed the 0.25 threshold and each led to a confidence vote (years 3 and 8). Three more (t8, t45, t95) have no expiry event because their target left office first (recall at t10, term end at t48 and at t96). The per-year table's low peaks of 0.002 (year 5) and 0.004 (year 21) are just the opening ticks of the t23 and t87 petitions, which went on to 0.1 and 0.196.

**Blank answers in the LLM probe.** These are the model's answers on an uncounted probe of about 10% of the whole population (abstainers included), not the electorate's blank voting. Blank shares by election year are 0.1321 (year 0), 0.5714 (year 2), 0.2917 (4), 0.4906 (8), 0.8936 (12), 0.4773 (16), 0.62 (20), 0.4423 (24) and 0.74 (28). Year 12 shows 42 of 47 (0.8936) blank, above the 0.5 `blank_invalidation_threshold`, which would have voided an election if these ballots were counted; they are not counted, so the 0 in `election_no_winner` and `election_invalidated` in every year says nothing about whether blank voting would have mattered. The counted legislative elections do record 12 blank ballots each (`legislative_result` blank_count).

**How the officeholder stood.** Legitimacy at the first tick of each term was 0.755 (31), 0.746 (13), 0.75 (499), 0.731 (398), 0.797 (388), 0.723 (446), 0.705 (31), 0.727 (289) and 0.678 (345). Legitimacy and the mandate-deviation behind it depend on `representative_response`, which the digest lists as **unverified** (known content-blind collapse signature); treat the trajectory shape, not exact values, as a finding. The coalition lifespan and cohabitation rate are also unverified (`coalition_decision`); here the coalition never changes anyway.

**Scandals** (6 in total, years 7, 9, 11, 23, 26, 28) each triggered a reaction from all 500 citizens, with a mean salience change between 0.0998 and 0.1685. The first (tick 30) is a content-blind collapse: 499 of 500 answers are exactly 0.1, so its 0.0998 mean says nothing about how citizens differed.
